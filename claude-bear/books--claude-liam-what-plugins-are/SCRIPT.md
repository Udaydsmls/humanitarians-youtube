## B00 — cold open (BrutalistHesitantWriter)
People think installing a plugin just makes Claude smarter — more facts,
same generalist. That's not it. A plugin makes Claude an equipped
specialist. So what does a plugin actually make Claude?

## Act I — The generalist, and the wrong guess

**NB01 — A smart friend, not an accountant** (source B01 — ANCHOR PLANTED)
Claude without plugins is a capable generalist. It can discuss any topic and
apply broad knowledge to your situation. Useful — but it's a smart friend
helping with your taxes. They'll try. They're not an accountant.

**NB02 — One well, dug deep** (source B02)
A plugin makes Claude the accountant — or the marketer, the sales
researcher, the contract reviewer. It trades a mile of shallow knowledge for
a well that goes deep in one domain.

**NB03 — Not just a smarter prompt** (source B03 — WRONG-GUESS)
It's tempting to think that's just a smarter prompt — more detail stuffed
into the same conversation. But a prompt can't log into your CRM, add a
slash-command shortcut to your menu, or hand a multi-step job to a
coordinating subagent. A plugin does all three.

## Act II — The four components

**NB04 — Expertise, auto-armed** (source B04)
Skills are the expertise — methodical frameworks, not generic tips. They
switch on automatically when they're relevant. Ask a marketing question
with marketing installed, and Claude just reaches for them.

**NB05 — Reach into your tools** (source B05)
Connectors are the reach. The sales plugin into your CRM, the data plugin
into your spreadsheets — so Claude works on your actual business data, not
hypothetical examples.

**NB06 — One slash, one job** (source B06)
Commands are the shortcuts. Slash-prospect-brief, and sales researches a
company. Slash-audit, and marketing tears apart a page. Type slash inside
Cowork to see them all.

**NB07 — Many steps, one answer** (source B07)
Subagents are the autonomy. Ask finance to build a model, and it quietly
coordinates the steps — gather, structure, run scenarios, compile — so you
get one clean result instead of a jumble.

## Act III — On your own work

**NB08 — Knowing becomes doing** (source B09+B10 merged)
That's the shift in one line: general knowledge becomes contextual
capability. Legal reviews the actual contract you're about to sign;
marketing drafts in your brand voice, from your real style guide — not a
hypothetical example.

**NB09 — The friend, and the pro** (source B11+B12 merged — ANCHOR PAYOFF)
Back to that friend and the accountant. It's the difference between asking
a well-read friend what to watch for in a contract, and asking someone who
reviews contracts for a living. That's the whole reason a plugin is worth
your time.

## Act IV — Free, open, and worth configuring

**NB10 — Open, so it bends** (source B13)
Anthropic released these as open source. For you that mostly means free —
no charge beyond your paid plan. But open buys three more things:
transparency instead of a black box, community fixes over time, and the
ability to adjust a plugin that almost fits.

**NB11 — Configured hits harder** (source B14)
Out of the box, the sales plugin is useful. Configured with your process,
your qualifying criteria, your positioning, it's dramatically more
powerful. Customization is the next chapter — and that's where the real
gain shows up.

## Act V — Both directions: what holds, what doesn't

**NB12 — Four hard limits** (source B15 — DIRECTION A)
Four limits, stated plainly. Plugins don't hand Claude perfect judgment.
They don't bypass its knowledge boundaries. They work only inside your
existing access — your credentials, your permissions, nothing new. And
they're tools, not replacements.

**NB13 — You keep the judgment** (source B16 — DIRECTION B)
Legal flags a scary clause; it can't weigh it against the deal. Finance
builds the model; it can't tell you the assumptions fit your industry.
Human judgment stays on the consequential calls. That caveat isn't a
disclaimer to skip — it's the instructions.

## Act VI — Choosing yours

**NB14 — Two or three** (source B17)
You don't need the whole catalog; most people run two or three. Start with
your primary function — whatever you spend your day on. Add your biggest
bottleneck — the thing you know you should do but don't. Then keep
productivity running underneath as the foundation.

Source B08 (a spark-line summary — "Skills are the expertise. The plugin is
