# CARRY-OUT — claude-for-legal--claude-liam-board-minutes

**The sentence:**

> Board minutes aren't official the moment they're drafted — they're official once the board approves them.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still true? Yes —
it names the actual boundary (board approval) without naming any tool's specific
steps, so it survives being repeated out of context.

**The wrong guess it's built to defeat:** that minutes are a transcript — a record of
everything that was said — so a fuller draft is a better draft. The reel falsifies
this with a concrete case (B01): minutes that captured every comment, hesitation, and
disagreement would be a liability, not a record — which is exactly why real board
minutes are deliberately limited to decisions, motions, and actions taken, not
discussion.

**Why this compresses the distinction that matters, not the topic:** the topic is
"board minutes"; the distinction is *draft vs. official record*. Every body beat exists
to make that distinction land — decisions-not-discussion (B01) is what the draft should
even contain, and the approval boundary (B02–B03) is what turns a correct draft into
the actual minutes.
