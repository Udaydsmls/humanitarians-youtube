## B00 — cold open (BrutalistHesitantWriter)
The sales plugin is only for professional salespeople. Who's it actually
for?

## Act I — Who it's actually for

**NB01 — Who it's actually for** (source B01+B02 merged)
Consultants, freelancers, agency owners, builders — people who need clients
but never studied pipeline management and never wanted to. Not a big sales
team with an administrator, though it handles that too. It's useful the
moment you need to research a prospect, prep a conversation, or follow up
after one.

**NB02 — At the kitchen table** (source B03 — WRONG-GUESS PAYOFF / ANCHOR
PLANTED, archival slot replaced with GRAPHIC per NO-GENAI/NO-PANTRY LAW)
Picture a freelancer with one lead they don't know how to work — no CRM, no
playbook, no manager looking over their shoulder. That's exactly where this
starts. Not a system for people who already have a sales process. A
collaborator for people who don't.

## Act II — Five kinds of help

**NB03 — Five kinds of help** (source B04)
Research. Prep. Follow-up. Pipeline. Outreach. Five things — and probably
work you're already doing badly, doing late, or not doing at all.

**NB04 — A structured briefing** (source B05)
Point it at a company or a person and get a structured briefing back — what
they do, recent news, needs you could address, key contacts. It works from
public information alone, and gets richer once you connect a CRM.

**NB05 — The prep sheet** (source B06)
For any client conversation, it builds a prep sheet: the relevant context,
talking points worth raising, questions worth asking. If you've kept notes
from before, it weaves those in too.

**NB06 — Not a template** (source B07)
Hand it your notes after a call and it drafts the follow-up. Not a
template — a message that references the specific things you discussed and
proposes a concrete next step.

**NB07 — Where things stand** (source B08)
Even if your pipeline is a spreadsheet, or just a list in your head, it
helps you see where everything stands — who's waiting on you, and what's
gone quiet.

## Act III — One deal, start to finish (the anchor, walked through)

**NB08 — An hour out** (source B10)
You've got a discovery call in an hour with an agency you barely know. So
you ask: research Meridian Design Studio — what they do, who they serve,
recent news, and what they might need from someone like me.

**NB09 — Walk in informed** (source B11+B12 merged)
A minute later you've got a one-page briefing you can scan in five. You
walk in informed instead of improvising — present, not scrambling, because
the research is already done. It doesn't sit at the table with you. It got
you ready for it.

**NB10 — Capture it fresh** (source B13)
The call ends. While it's fresh, you dump what happened — their timeline,
their real concern, who actually decides. Five minutes of notes, captured
now instead of forgotten by Friday.

**NB11 — Three days to five minutes** (source B14 — ANCHOR PAYOFF)
From those notes it drafts a follow-up that hits every point and suggests a
logical next step, in a warm, professional tone. The email that would've
sat on your list for three days is done in five minutes.

## Act IV — The rest of the pipeline

**NB12 — Honest, no manager** (source B15+B18 merged, archival slot
replaced with GRAPHIC)
Hand it your whole prospect list and ask the hard questions: which
conversations need a nudge, what's gone cold, where this week's energy
should go. It's like having a sales manager who keeps you honest about
follow-through — without the awkwardness of actually having one.

**NB13 — Names with context** (source B16)
Need new names? Ask it to scout a market — agencies in a region that might
need what you offer — and it comes back with a list that has context, not
just company names. You decide who's worth pursuing.

**NB14 — Human, not spammy** (source B17)
And when you reach out cold, it drafts something that references their
actual situation and offers a conversation — not a hard sell. Personal
enough to feel human, structured enough to say something worth reading.

## Act V — Configured, not required

**NB15 — Three things, then a CRM if you want** (source B19+B20 merged)
Tell it three things: what you sell, how your process works, who your
ideal client is — and it stops guessing and starts sounding like you.
Connect a CRM, even a free one, and its research gets richer still. But it
isn't required.

**NB16 — Feed it your wins** (source B21)
When you close a deal, tell it what worked — what resonated, which
objection came up, what sealed it. Over time that builds institutional
knowledge that sharpens every future suggestion.

## Act VI — What it won't do

**NB17 — Take the chores, not the room** (source B22+B23 merged, archival
slot replaced with GRAPHIC — BOTH DIRECTIONS, part 1)
But don't let it automate the relationship — it's excellent at preparation
and follow-through, not a replacement for being genuinely present in the
conversation. Hand it the admin instead: activity logging, note formatting,
follow-up scheduling. The chores you skip when busy, so you can focus on
people.

**NB18 — It only knows what you tell it** (source B24 — BOTH DIRECTIONS,
part 2, archival slot replaced with GRAPHIC)
And keep your notes current — it can only work with what it knows. Sparse
notes make sparse briefings. Those five minutes after each call pay
dividends in every conversation after.

## Close

**BCRY — carry-out**
The sales plugin does the homework — research, prep, follow-up, pipeline.
It never has the conversation for you: the relationship stays yours.

## Handoff prompt (BHTF, read aloud)

> "I have a client conversation coming up. Here's who it's with: [company
> or person]. Research them from public information — what they do, recent
> news, and what they might need from someone who offers [what you do].
> Then give me a one-page prep sheet: the context I should know, three
> talking points, and three questions to ask. End with the one thing most
> likely to move this conversation forward."

Why it's worth running: it's the same one-page-briefing workflow NB08-NB09
walks through with a stand-in prospect — running it on your own next call
is the fastest way to see whether it saves you the five minutes it claims.
