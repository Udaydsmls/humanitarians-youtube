# Claude, Shipping — The Product Plugin

People assume the product plugin decides what to build next for you. It
doesn't — it narrows the options. Big teams have a product manager asking who
a feature is for, what problem it solves, and what's deliberately not being
built; build solo and nobody asks those questions. This walks the whole loop:
the discipline it installs, the spec and stories it turns a rough idea into,
how it separates the loudest feedback from the most common need, and where it
stops — it ranks a shortlist, but the call at the desk stays yours.

## YOUR TURN

I'm building my product solo and I have more feature ideas than time. Here
are the features I'm considering for next quarter: [list them]. I have
about [N] hours a month to build. My users are mostly [who they are and
what they use it for]. Help me prioritize: (1) which should I build first,
(2) which can wait, and (3) which is a distraction dressed up as a good
idea — and tell me why for each.

Then push back on its ranking; the argument is the point.

---

## Deliberately not claimed

- **No plugin version number or specific UI labels invented.** Behavior is
  described generically ("connect your analytics," "point it at three
  months of feedback"), not tied to a dated interface.
- **No claim it replaces judgment.** It ranks and drafts; the final call —
  what to build, what to skip — stays a stated human decision, never
  softened into a sales pitch.
- **No accusation and no design critique.** The reel explains what the
  plugin does and where its edge sits; it never judges why Anthropic built
  it that way — that's Teardown territory, out of scope for this register.
