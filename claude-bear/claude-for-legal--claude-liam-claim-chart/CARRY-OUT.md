# CARRY-OUT — claude-for-legal--claude-liam-claim-chart

**The line (written first, GATE C):**

> The point of a claim chart isn't a full grid — it's a pin cite for what's
> supported, and a flag, element by element, for what's missing.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still
true? Yes — it compresses the one distinction the reel is built to land
(the chart's job is citation + gap detection, not the appearance of
completeness), not the topic (claim charts generally).

**The wrong guess it defeats:** that a chart with every cell filled in is
the strong version — a full grid reads as "the case is covered." A filled
cell only shows that a citation was found and pinned to a page; it says
nothing about whether that citation actually proves the element. The
anchor (one claim, two elements: "a locking mechanism" gets a citation
straight to the product manual, page twelve; "a temperature sensor" gets
no citation at all — a gap, flagged on purpose) falsifies the guess
directly: the useful signal on this chart isn't the filled cell, it's the
one that couldn't be filled.
