# Claude, Scouting — The Research Plugin

Market research is the thing every operator knows they should do and never
finds time for — an afternoon lost to open tabs, printouts, and trying to
hold a stack of sources in your head at once. Point Claude at the same pile
and it does in about an hour what used to take a day: not a summary of each
source, but synthesis — the themes, the conflicts, and the throughline no
single article stated on its own. Aimed at a market, the same engine scouts
competitors onto a positioning map, tracks every citation back to its
source, and flags the coverage gaps you haven't noticed yet. Name your
actual decision and the output sharpens to it. The one honest catch: every
finding is a lead to verify, never a verdict to trust outright — a result
that matches what you expected isn't proof, and one that surprises you isn't
disproof either. The scouting is Claude's; the judgment stays yours.

## YOUR TURN

I'm considering entering [market or opportunity]. Act as my market scout:
(1) size it and name who's already there, (2) map the main competitors on
positioning and where the gaps are, (3) list the top three things customers
in this space actually need, and (4) flag every claim you're unsure about
so I know what to verify before I commit.

Run that today, on the market you're actually weighing, not the video's
example.

## Deliberately not claimed

No plugin name/version number and no named competitors or markets —
"Rival Co," "a new market," and "the empty quadrant" stand in for whatever
you're actually researching. No claim that the plugin replaces due
diligence or expert judgment: the reel is explicit that findings are leads
to verify, not verdicts, and that a result matching your expectation is no
more proof than a surprising one is disproof — the scouting is Claude's, the
judgment stays yours.
