# Claude, In Order — The Productivity Plugin

People assume the productivity plugin just tracks your tasks for you. It
doesn't — it runs your whole day. Every other Cowork plugin picks a domain;
this one picks you. This walks the whole loop: the five moves it covers
(tasks, meetings, email, schedule, notes), why it works the moment you
install it and needs nothing connected, what a calendar adds when you do
connect one, how it learns your method and compounds over sessions, and why
it ends up as the layer most people never turn off.

## YOUR TURN

I just installed the productivity plugin and I want it to run my day. Ask
me what I spend most of my time on and where my time leaks. Ask how I
already prioritize — deadlines, an urgency-importance matrix, whatever I
use. Then draft me a morning-briefing routine and a Friday weekly-review
routine built around my real schedule, and tell me the one thing to put in
global instructions so you remember it every session.

Why it's worth running: naming your real schedule and method out loud is
what turns a one-off chat into a standing setup.

## Deliberately not claimed

- **No plugin version number or specific UI labels invented.** Behavior is
  described generically ("connect a calendar," "point it at a wall of
  unread mail"), not tied to a dated interface.
- **No claim it replaces existing tools.** It's explicit: connective
  tissue, not a new to-do app and not a calendar replacement.
- **No accusation and no design critique.** The reel explains what the
  plugin does and where it sits in the stack; it never judges why Anthropic
  built it that way — that's Teardown territory, out of scope for this
  register.
