## B00 — cold open (BrutalistHesitantWriter)
People assume the productivity plugin just tracks your tasks for you. It
doesn't — it runs your whole day: tasks, meetings, email and notes
together. So what does it actually do?

## Act I — It picks you, not a domain

**NB01 — It picks you** (source B01+B02 merged)
Every other plugin in the collection picks a domain — sales, legal, finance.
It points outward, deep into one field. The productivity plugin points
inward, at the day you actually work through. Same engine, opposite aim: it
picks you.

**NB02 — Fluent in you** (source B03)
It doesn't make Claude an expert in something. It makes Claude fluent in
you — how your day is structured, what you owe people, what's on fire right
now. That's why most people install it first.

## Act II — Five moves

**NB03 — Five moves, not five apps** (source B04 — ANCHOR PLANTED)
Five moves, mostly: track tasks, prep for meetings, triage email, stay aware
of your schedule, synthesize your notes. Not five apps — five things Claude
can now do inside the conversation you're already having.

**NB04 — You talk, it organizes** (source B05)
Task management is the base. Tell Claude what you need to do in plain
language and it structures it. Tell it what's urgent and it sequences your
day. No forms, no fields — you talk, it organizes.

**NB05 — Scattered becomes summary** (source B06)
Note synthesis is the quiet one. Information scattered across
conversations, documents, and meetings gets pulled into one coherent
summary — the stuff you'd normally lose between tabs.

**NB06 — Not a to-do app** (source B07 — WRONG-GUESS CORRECTION)
One caution up front: this isn't a new to-do app, and it doesn't replace
your calendar. It's connective tissue — it makes Claude better at helping
you work the tools you already have.

## Act III — Setup, or none at all

**NB07 — Live on install** (source B08)
The productivity plugin works the second you install it. No accounts to
link, no connectors to authorize. That's rare in this collection, and it's
why it's a safe first install.

**NB08 — Still useful, sharper connected** (source B09+B10 merged — BOTH
DIRECTIONS)
One optional add-on: your calendar. Without it, the plugin is still useful —
it just can't see your time. Connect one, the standard three steps, and
Claude crosses a line: now it knows what's committed, what's free, and where
your focus blocks can go.

## Act IV — What a morning looks like

**NB09 — Just ask your day** (source B11+B12 merged)
Picture the first move of the day: before the tabs, before the noise, you
just ask what your day looks like. What's my day look like? I have a client
call at ten and a proposal due Friday — what should I prioritize? Plain
speech, real constraints.

**NB10 — Three streams, one plan** (source B13)
If your calendar's connected, Claude fuses three streams — your schedule,
the priorities you just named, and tasks left over from earlier
conversations — into one actionable plan. Not a to-do list. A plan for the
day, sequenced.

**NB11 — Prep before the room** (source B14+B15 merged)
Second workflow: before a meeting that matters, you ask Claude to prepare
you. It pulls context from your files, past conversations, and general
knowledge into a briefing — you walk in already knowing who they are, what
they want, and what to raise.

**NB12 — Sort, draft, review** (source B16)
Third: the inbox. Point it at a wall of unread mail and it sorts by
urgency — what needs a reply today, what can wait, what to archive — and
drafts the easy ones for you to approve. Careful processing collapses into
quick review.

**NB13 — Capture, don't switch** (source B17)
And the small one that saves you daily: capture without breaking flow.
Something comes up mid-task — you say, add a follow-up with Sarah, due
Thursday. It's logged, and you never left what you were doing.

## Act V — It learns your shape

**NB14 — Your method, adopted** (source B18)
Tell Claude how you already work and it adapts — it doesn't impose a
method. Say you prioritize with an urgency-versus-importance matrix, and
that's how it lays your options out: your rule, not its default.

**NB15 — It compounds** (source B19)
And it compounds. The more Claude knows about your recurring commitments,
ongoing projects, and working patterns, the more useful it gets. You don't
front-load any of it — it accrues one conversation at a time.

**NB16 — Set once, remembered** (source B20)
The shortcut for that is global instructions — persistent context that
applies to every session: your role, your typical week, how you like
information presented. Set it once; Claude remembers.

## Act VI — The foundation layer

**NB17 — Underneath it all** (source B21)
Think in layers. Your domain plugins — sales, legal, whatever you run — sit
on top. Productivity sits underneath all of them, holding the day together
while they do the specialist work.

**NB18 — The base holds** (source B22+B23 merged)
You swap specialists in and out as the work changes, but the base holds,
session after session, carrying your tasks and context forward. In
practice it feels less like an app and more like a desk that's already
tidy when you sit down.

**NB19 — Your operating system** (source B24 — ANCHOR PAYOFF)
Add it up and you get the pitch in the title. One generalist under a few
specialists, doing the same five moves from the top of this reel — task
tracking, meeting prep, email triage, schedule awareness, note synthesis —
now running underneath everything else. That's the operating system.

## Close

**BCRY — carry-out**
The productivity plugin doesn't specialize in a domain — it specializes in
you, working the moment you install it and getting sharper the more it
knows how you work. That's why it's the layer most people never turn off.

## Handoff prompt (BHTF, read aloud)

> "I just installed the productivity plugin and I want it to run my day.
> Ask me what I spend most of my time on and where my time leaks. Ask how I
> already prioritize — deadlines, an urgency-importance matrix, whatever I
> use. Then draft me a morning-briefing routine and a Friday weekly-review
> routine built around my real schedule, and tell me the one thing to put
> in global instructions so you remember it every session."

Why it's worth running: it's the same "talk, don't configure" move NB04-NB16
walk through — naming your real schedule and method out loud is what turns
a one-off chat into a standing setup, and the last part is the whole trick:
it turns today's answer into tomorrow's default.
