## B00 — cold open (BrutalistHesitantWriter)
People assume the marketing plugin is just a faster way to get content
written. It isn't — it plans, analyzes, and refines like a collaborator
would. So what does it actually change?

## Act I — What marketing actually is

**NB01 — You're already doing marketing** (source B01)
If you're running a business, you're running marketing — whether or not
you'd call yourself a marketer. Content needs making, campaigns need
planning, competitors need watching. Somewhere in the middle, you decide
what to say, to whom, and where.

**NB02 — More than a drafting tool** (source B02+B03 merged — WRONG-GUESS
PAYOFF)
Most tools stop at "write me a blog post." This one plans, analyzes, and
refines — it understands strategy, not just content generation. The
alternative is the brief you scrawl between meetings, half a thought
handed to whoever's free. A collaborator that already knows your brand
changes that math.

## Act II — Five things it does

**NB03 — Five sides, one install** (source B04)
Five capabilities come under one install: creating content, planning
campaigns, reading competitors, interpreting performance, and holding your
brand consistent. Not five apps — five sides of one collaborator.

**NB04 — Content creation** (source B05)
Content creation isn't just "write me a post." It's structured
development — briefs, outlines, drafts, and variations. Email sequences,
social calendars, landing-page copy, ad variants, each shaped by your
configured voice and audience.

**NB05 — Campaign planning** (source B06)
Campaign planning runs from the initial brief to channel strategy. Tell it
what you're launching, who you're reaching, and what you want them to do —
it helps build the messaging framework, choose channels, and plan the
execution.

**NB06 — Reads the competition** (source B07)
It also watches the competition — how rivals position themselves, who
they're talking to, what they claim. Then it finds the gap: the angle
everyone's ignoring, the audience nobody's addressing.

**NB07 — Reads the numbers with you** (source B08)
Connect your analytics and it reads the campaign data with you. Not just
"here are the numbers" — "here's what the numbers suggest you do next."
Interpretation, not another dashboard.

**NB08 — Holds the brand line** (source B09)
And it holds the line on brand. Configure your voice, tone, and
terminology once, and it enforces them across everything it produces —
which matters most exactly when you're shipping volume and consistency
starts to slip.

## Act III — The setup that pays for itself

**NB09 — Three inputs, once** (source B10)
Out of the box it works. But the real gain hides in customization — and
customization is three inputs: your brand voice, your audience, and what
you actually sell.

**NB10 — The anchor: specificity is the lever** (source B11 — ANCHOR
PLANTED)
Specificity is the lever. "Professionals" is nearly useless.
"Small-business owners considering AI tools but not technical" — that's
something it can aim at. The narrower you describe the target, the sharper
everything downstream.

**NB11 — Voice, calibrated by example** (source B12)
Voice is calibrated by example, not description. Show it a few paragraphs
you've written in your tone, and tell it who you're reaching. It
calibrates to that — not to a generic house style.

**NB12 — The anchor pays off: fifteen minutes** (source B13 — ANCHOR
PAYOFF)
Fifteen minutes of this changes the output. Content comes back closer to
your voice, ideas grounded in your real business, competitive analysis
focused on the players who actually matter. Eighty-percent-right on the
first draft beats fifty-percent-right, by a lot.

## Act IV — Five workflows

**NB13 — The content calendar** (source B14)
Instead of staring at a blank month, say: build me a calendar — two posts
a week, a weekly newsletter, two blog pieces, for this audience, on these
themes. It returns a structured calendar with a specific idea in every
slot. You're editing a plan, not inventing one.

**NB14 — The email sequence** (source B15+B16 merged)
Say you're launching a new service next month. Ask for a five-email
sequence aimed at subscribers who know you but have never bought
anything — it drafts the whole arc, subject lines, body copy, and calls to
action, each email building on the one before. You add the personal
anecdotes it can't know, and the sequence is ready to load.

**NB15 — Repurposing** (source B17)
Repurposing is the tedious work everyone skips. One long blog post becomes
five social posts, a newsletter intro, and a short video script — from a
single source. This is where it saves real hours.

**NB16 — The monthly reckoning** (source B18+B19 merged)
And the monthly reckoning: which pieces landed, which fell flat, is there
a pattern? Feed it your metrics and it goes past the numbers to what they
mean for next month's strategy. Across all five workflows, the shift is
the same — you're editing a plan, not staring at a blank page.

## Act V — Four habits, and the honest limit

**NB17 — Four habits** (source B20+B21 merged)
Four habits separate the people who get value from the people who get
slop: frontload your brand configuration, ask for variations, delegate the
formats you hate, and review everything. Everyone has formats they
dread — captions, subject lines, meta descriptions. Hand those over
specifically, and keep your creative energy for the work only you can do.

**NB18 — Options, not answers** (source B22)
Ask for options, not answers. "Give me five angles on this headline" beats
"write me a headline" almost every time — the plugin is at its best
producing a spread you choose from, not one guess.

**NB19 — What it won't do** (source B23 — BOTH DIRECTIONS)
But review everything that goes public. It drafts well; your touch is what
makes the work yours. It won't replace your CMO — it just means you're
never again starting from a brief scrawled between meetings. You keep the
judgment; it takes the grind.

## Close

**BCRY — carry-out**
The marketing plugin turns Claude from a content generator into a
collaborator — it plans, watches competitors, and reads your numbers with
you. But it only gets specific once you do, and the judgment stays yours.
