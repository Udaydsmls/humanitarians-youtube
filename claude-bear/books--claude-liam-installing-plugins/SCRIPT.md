## B00 — cold open (BrutalistHesitantWriter)
People think installing a plugin is the whole job. It isn't — the plugin
arrives generic. So how do you actually make it customized, for your
business?

## Act I — Installing is easy

**NB01 — Browse by function** (source B01)
Open the plugin section and you'll find a library — an official collection
plus community additions. Each listing says what it does and what it
covers. Browse by function and find the ones that fit your work.

**NB02 — Click, and capabilities appear** (source B02+B03 merged)
Click install and it downloads, activates, and is ready — no setup wizard.
And once it's in, its capabilities just appear: skills switch on when
they're relevant, slash commands show up in your menu, connectors sit
ready to configure. Nothing to wire up first.

**NB03 — Shared plugins upload too** (source B04)
A colleague shares a plugin, or you find one from the community — it
arrives as a file. Click upload, pick it, and it installs the same way.
Same couple of minutes as any other install.

## Act II — Making it yours

**NB04 — You don't do generic** (source B05 — WRONG-GUESS)
Out of the box, a plugin runs on sensible defaults — generic best practice
for its domain. Useful. But you don't do generic work. You have your own
process, your own terms, your own way in.

**NB05 — A conversation, not config** (source B06 — WRONG-GUESS PAYOFF)
Click customize and it doesn't open a settings panel — it starts a
conversation. It asks about your workflow, your preferences, your tools.
You answer in plain language, and the plugin adjusts from what you said.

**NB06 — Ask, answer, calibrate** (source B07)
The pattern never changes: it asks, you answer, it calibrates. Ask again,
answer again, it tightens — the same loop whether you spend two questions
or twenty.

**NB07 — Your call, your depth** (source B08)
How far you take it is your call. Run the defaults — fine, still useful.
Or spend a few minutes and get something that feels built for your
business. It scales with how central this domain is to your day.

## Act III — A real customization

**NB08 — What do you do?** (source B09 — ANCHOR PLANTED)
Say you install a marketing plugin and click customize. First question:
what does your business do, and who are your customers? You answer — a
small web design studio, clients are professional-services firms with no
marketing team of their own.

**NB09 — The studio, and its clients** (source B10+B11 merged)
This is the studio the plugin is about to learn — one person, a few
screens, real clients. And these are the clients it now understands:
accountants, lawyers, financial advisors who need a modern site but have
no marketing team of their own.

**NB10 — Voice, audience, formats** (source B12)
A few more answers — voice is professional but approachable, no jargon;
formats are social posts and a monthly newsletter. The plugin locks it in:
the right audience, the right voice, the right formats.

**NB11 — The anchor returns, tuned** (source B13 — ANCHOR PAYOFF)
Now the output changes. Ask for a content calendar and you get posts and
newsletter topics — not viral video scripts or press releases. A
competitive scan targets other web studios, not enterprise agencies.
Generic advice becomes advice from someone who knows your business.

## Act IV — Connecting your tools

**NB12 — Self-contained or connected** (source B14)
Some plugins are self-contained — useful out of the box, connected to
nothing. Others get dramatically more useful the moment you connect them
to the tools you already run.

**NB13 — Connected reaches further** (source B15+B16 merged)
A sales plugin researches a prospect from public information alone —
decent. Connect it to your CRM and it pulls in your relationship history
and every past conversation. Same shape for data: give it files, or
connect your live database and it works with your actual numbers
directly — no exporting.

**NB14 — Authorize, then reach** (source B17)
Connecting is the standard login flow — authorize access the same way
you'd link any other app. And it's optional: start without connections,
add them later once you can see the value.

## Act V — Living with them

**NB15 — Off without erasing** (source B18)
Want it to focus? Disable a plugin without removing it — your
customization stays preserved, waiting. Flip it back on when you need it.
Nothing you taught it is lost.

**NB16 — They keep improving** (source B19)
Plugins improve over time — features get added, issues get fixed, and
updates are handled for you. Worth checking in occasionally to see what's
new that you might actually use.

**NB17 — Two or three, active** (source B20)
Loading up on plugins doesn't slow it down or confuse it — but in practice
most people keep two or three active. The rest stay installed and
disabled, ready the moment a job calls for them.

**NB18 — Local to your machine** (source B21 — BOUNDARY: holds here, flips there)
One thing worth knowing: your plugins and everything you taught them live
on your machine. Work from a second computer and you set them up again
there — the customization is personal, and it doesn't follow you
automatically.

**NB19 — Test it once** (source B22)
After installing, run one test. Type a slash to see its commands, or just
ask it to do the job — build a content calendar for next month, say. A
tuned plugin answers noticeably more structured and aware of your world.
