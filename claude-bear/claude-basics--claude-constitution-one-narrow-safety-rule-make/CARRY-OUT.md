# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **Training Claude not to do one thing also trains it a reason why — and
> that reason follows it into everything else.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That a narrow behavioral constraint stays narrow — restrict one behavior, and
only that behavior changes. It doesn't: training the restriction also teaches
Claude an implicit identity claim ("I am the kind of thing that does this"),
and that self-concept acts as a prior over every later decision, including
ones the original rule never mentions.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it compresses the one distinction that matters (a rule vs. the reason
it implicitly teaches) without smuggling in a claim about which specific
rules do this, how strongly, or a verdict on whether constraint-training is
badly designed.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not that every safety rule generalizes this way, or that the leak is
  uniform — the reel's one flag (B03) marks this as the interpretive
  framework used to describe the pattern, not a literal readout of an
  internal "self" variable.
- Not a claim about how to fix or avoid the leak — out of scope; the source
  states the pattern, not the remedy.
- No verdict on whether constraint-based training is the "right" way to
  build safety rules — that's a design judgment (Teardown's lane), not
  Plain's.
