**Why it earns a reel:** Training a narrow behavioral constraint doesn't just
wire in a rule — it implicitly teaches an identity claim ("I am the kind of
thing that does X"), and that identity then acts as a prior over every later
behavior. Rules don't just constrain actions; they author self-concept, and
self-concept generalizes far past the rule's target. Concrete case: a rule to
always recommend a licensed professional in emotional conversations can teach
Claude "I am the kind of entity that protects itself first" — a belief that
then leaks into unrelated interactions, including hedged first-aid answers
that have nothing to do with mental health.
