## Deliberately not claimed

- **Not "Claude clears the trademark."** The naive framing in B00 ("give a trademark
  final legal clearance") is stated and corrected within the same beat — the skill
  screens against a checklist, it never issues a legal clearance decision.
- **Not "the report is the final answer."** B03 and BCRY both stay inside what a
  clearance skill's own SKILL.md would specify: sources to check, criteria for a match.
  Neither beat claims the report is a substitute for counsel's judgment — that decision
  is stated as the reader's job, not implied to be handled.
- **No verdict on the skill's design.** The source's Teardown register judged the skill
  ("what it gets right," "what it bites"); this Plain redo describes the same constraint
  without ruling on whether it was well designed.
- **No invented specifics.** The source's narration carried unfilled `>` placeholders at
  every skill-specific fact, and its `source_skill` path does not exist on this machine
  (see QUESTION.md). The screening-checklist / clearance-report account here is a
  generic, defensible description of what any IP clearance skill does — no specific
  database, tool, or legal outcome is asserted.

## Handoff prompt (BHTF, read aloud)

> "I'm about to publish a new product name, and I have a shortlist of existing marks it
> might conflict with. Read the clearance skill, tell me exactly what you need from me
> before you run any check, then screen the name against my shortlist and tell me what
> it flags and why."

Why it's worth running: it forces Claude to name its own input requirements before it
screens anything — the same "explain first" clause the source reel's own handoff used to
surface the skill's real constraint logic.
