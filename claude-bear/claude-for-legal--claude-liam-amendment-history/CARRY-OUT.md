**The line (written first, GATE C):**

> The contract in force today isn't the original, and it isn't the
> amendments — it's the original with each clause replaced by its most
> recent edit. Check clause by clause, not document by document.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still
true? Yes — it compresses the one distinction the reel is built to land
(current terms = original + last edit per clause), not the topic
(contract amendments generally).

**The wrong guess it defeats:** that the original signed document is still
the authority, and the amendments are separate paperwork attached to it.
The original only ever says what it said on day one — it has no way of
knowing anything was signed after it. The anchor (a lease's notice period:
30 days in the original, stretched to 60 by the first amendment, cut back
to 45 by the second) falsifies the guess directly: reading the original
alone gives 30, which is not what's in force. What's in force is 45 — the
last edit to that specific clause, not the original and not the sum of
every edit.
