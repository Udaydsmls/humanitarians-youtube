# CARRY-OUT — claude-for-legal--claude-liam-client-intake

**The line (written first, GATE C):**

> Client intake isn't writing down the story — it's catching the conflict
> before you've heard too much of it. A gatekeeping question that comes
> after the details arrived too late.

**Test:** if someone repeats only this in a meeting next week, is it still
true? Yes — it compresses the one distinction the reel is built to land
(gatekeeping-before-story, not story-then-gatekeeping), not the topic
(legal client intake generally).

**The wrong guess it defeats:** that intake means getting the story down
accurately — a careful, complete summary of what the prospective client
says. A flawless summary is worthless, and worse than worthless, if it
turns out the firm already represents the other side in this exact
dispute: the notes get sealed and the meeting should never have gone that
far. What intake actually has to nail first is one question — who is on
the other side — asked before the story is allowed to run, not after (the
anchor: a landlord dispute where the very next question, regardless of how
much story has already poured out, is the landlord's name, checked against
the firm's current clients before another word about the case).
