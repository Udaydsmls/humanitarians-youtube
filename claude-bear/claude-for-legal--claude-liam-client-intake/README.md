# Claude, Client Intake.

Someone new to legal client intake naturally assumes the job is getting the
story down carefully — a complete, accurate summary of what the prospective
client says. But a flawless summary is worthless, and worse, if it turns out
the firm already represents the other side in this exact dispute: the notes
get sealed and the meeting should never have gone that far. What intake
actually has to nail first is one question, asked before the story is
allowed to run — who is on the other side. Watch the anchor: a caller
launches straight into a landlord dispute, and the very next question anyway
is the landlord's name, checked against the firm's current clients before
another word about the case. A match doesn't end the story — it starts a
different one: conflict flagged before a single privileged detail was on
record. But no match yet isn't a clean bill either, and clearing conflicts
doesn't mean taking the case.

## YOUR TURN

"I'm about to take an intake call with a new client. Before I let them tell
me the whole story, help me write a short list of questions I need answered
first — starting with who's on the other side — so I catch a conflict
before I've heard anything I can't unhear."

This forces the same reordering the reel is built around — putting the one
gatekeeping question ahead of the story instead of after it.

## Deliberately not claimed

This reel is a reconstruction: the source it redoes had never had its
skill-specific facts written (its narration carried literal unfilled
template placeholders, and the SKILL.md file it pointed at doesn't exist on
this machine). Rather than invent specific product claims, this script
describes the generic shape of a legal-intake conflict check — fixed order,
conflict question first — not any single tool's documented behavior. It
never claims documentation doesn't matter, only that the conflict check has
to come before it.
