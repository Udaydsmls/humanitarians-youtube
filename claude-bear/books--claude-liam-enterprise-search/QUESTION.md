**Why it earns a reel:** any company running more than a year has a
retrieval problem, not a knowledge problem — the pricing rationale, the
onboarding process, the vendor terms are all written down *somewhere*, just
not where you're looking. Normal search doesn't help because it matches the
file's name, not its content: call a document "Q3 notes" when it actually
holds the reason you switched payment processors, and searching "payment
processor" by filename finds nothing. Enterprise search reads what's
actually inside your documents — across your drive, wiki, folders, and chat
at once — and can pull the matching content straight into the conversation,
so Claude reasons from your own decisions instead of generic advice. The
one line that matters most: it is bounded strictly by your existing access.
It cannot reach anything you couldn't already open yourself — it only
reaches it faster.

**Naive framing (B00, corrected on screen):** "Can Claude search everywhere
to find it?" → corrects "everywhere" to "only what I can see" (enterprise

**Body facts carried from source (unchanged):**
- the buried-answer problem: knowledge exists but is scattered across tools
  and people, not missing
- filename search matches names, not content — the "Q3 notes" file holding a
  payment-processor decision is invisible to a filename search for "payment
  processor"
- enterprise search reads inside documents' actual content and searches
  every connected source (drive, wiki, shared folders, chat) at once, from a
  single plain-language question, no query syntax
- it can pull the found content into the conversation, so answers are
  grounded in the company's own documents rather than generic best practice
- worked examples from source: institutional memory (why a past decision was
  made), client context, policy lookup, meeting prep, avoiding contradicting
  a past commitment
- bounded strictly by the user's existing access/permissions — never a way
  to see something you couldn't already open; nothing indexed or connected
  is searchable until you point it there, and first-time indexing takes a
  while
- the payoff compounds: naming things clearly and documenting decisions as
  they're made is what future search can actually find
