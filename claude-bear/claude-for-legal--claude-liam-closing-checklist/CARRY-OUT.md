# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **closing-checklist runs the steps written in its SKILL.md, the same way
> every time — not legal judgment, and nothing the file doesn't say.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That Claude draws on its own general legal knowledge to decide what belongs
on a closing checklist. It doesn't — the skill's whole behavior comes from
one file, `SKILL.md`, read in order and executed step by step. Ask it to do
something the file doesn't cover, and it isn't improvising past the file; it
has simply stopped at the edge of what the file specifies.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — it compresses the one distinction that matters (the file's written
steps drive the output, not judgment) and states the boundary that follows
from it (same input → same output; nothing outside the spec) without needing
a second sentence.

## What it deliberately does not say

- **Not a verdict on the design.** The source's B03/BVDT framed this in
  Teardown language — "the interesting constraint," "what it gets right /
  where it bites." Plain keeps the same facts (spec-bound execution,
  repeatable results, a hard edge at what the file says) but states them as
  mechanism, never a critique of whether `closing-checklist` was well
  designed.
- **Not a claim about what specific legal work `closing-checklist` performs**
  beyond building and tracking a transaction's closing checklist. The
  source's own narration left its "job" beat and its Your Turn prompt as
  unfilled `>` placeholders — the specific legal task was never recorded on
  this machine. This reel does not invent one; it states only what the
  source confirms about the mechanism.
- **Not that Claude goes silent outside the spec.** The reel states what
  happens when a request falls outside the `SKILL.md`'s steps — that step
  isn't part of the run — not that Claude refuses to respond or explain why.
