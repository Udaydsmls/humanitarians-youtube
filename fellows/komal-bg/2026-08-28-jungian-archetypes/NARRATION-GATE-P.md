## B01 — BLUF
Here is the whole idea in one breath. Jungian archetypes are recurring character patterns humans already understand. Marketers use them so a brand feels like one person, not a committee. An A.I. tool can make that personality show up in every headline, email, and campaign — not just a workshop slide.

## B02 — Origins
Carl Jung called them archetypes — characters living in what he named the collective unconscious. Not invented by one ad agency. Shared. The Hero, the Sage, the Caregiver — you recognize them before anyone defines them.

## B03 — Twelve
Branding borrowed the wheel. Mark and Pearson mapped twelve archetypes for business — Innocent, Sage, Explorer, Hero, Outlaw, Magician, Everyman, Lover, Jester, Caregiver, Creator, Ruler. Pick a primary. Name a shadow. Every downstream choice — copy tone, visual palette, feature emphasis — follows from that character.

## B04 — Marketing
Why marketers care: an archetype is a shortcut to consistency. Tone of voice — a Sage does not joke like a Jester. Positioning — a Hero challenges; a Caregiver protects. Every touchpoint reads as the same character — ads, website, support, social — because the archetype is the operating system underneath.

## B05 — AI tool
Without a tool, archetypes die in a workshop deck. With an A.I. archetype engine, the theory becomes daily practice: classify which character the brand is playing, draft copy that stays in character, and flag lines that drift off-archetype before they ship.

## B06 — Tell
Design tell. A gimmick runs a personality quiz and forgets it. A mechanism picks an archetype, writes forcing functions, and lets A.I. enforce them. If the archetype does not change what you ship, it is decoration.

## B07 — Verdict
Let's recap with Claude. Jung named the shared patterns. Mark and Pearson turned them into a twelve-archetype brand wheel. Marketing uses them for tone, positioning, and consistency. An A.I. archetype tool classifies, drafts, and flags — so the character survives contact with a real campaign calendar. Design judgment: archetype as operating system, not a quiz.
