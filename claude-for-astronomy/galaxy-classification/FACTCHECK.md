# FACTCHECK — *Learning What the Crowd Would Say.*

**Reel:** `claude-hai-galaxy-classification` (ai-explainer, claude-hai) · **Ep. 04**

## Provenance of this verification — read first

Unlike Ep. 03, which inherited a prior fact-check, **this episode's claims were
verified from scratch during the build (2026-08-15)** against primary sources —
published papers and the Galaxy Zoo project's own data release pages. Each row
below carries the figure as the source states it and the URL it came from.

Two things a reviewer should know:

1. **Every number in the narration is a published figure.** The one exception is
   B07's worked example (63 of 100 volunteers calling a galaxy barred), which is
   an *invented illustration* of how a vote fraction is formed. It is spoken as
   "**say** sixty three of a hundred" and captioned on screen as illustrative.
2. **Every galaxy image in this reel is synthetic**, generated by
   `assets/gen_galaxies.py`. No claim rests on them; they illustrate the
   morphology classes the narration names. Scenes that show them caption them.

**Money status:** $0.00. Kokoro (local) for voice, Manim + Remotion (local) for
every visual, procedurally generated imagery. No paid API, no licensed asset.

## Verified, then deliberately NOT used

- **Hanny's Voorwerp** (found in 2007 by volunteer Hanny van Arkel; a quasar
  ionization echo near IC 2497) and the **Green Pea** galaxies — both genuine
  volunteer discoveries, both verified
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanny's_Voorwerp);
  [Lintott et al. 2009, MNRAS 399, 129](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.5304);
  [*Ideas for Citizen Science in Astronomy*, arXiv:1409.4291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.4291)).
  **Cut on purpose.** "Volunteers found the thing the machine could not name" is
  precisely Ep. 03's closing rule, and running it again one episode later would
  make the series repeat its own punchline. This episode's limit is a different
  one — the model's ceiling is the crowd, not the crowd's blind spot.
- **The >50 million classifications / >150,000 participants in year one** figure
  — true and sourced, but the beat only needs one rate and one repeat-look number,
  and stacking three volume figures made B04 a data dump.
- **The "too much data for humans" framing** — cut by series design, not by
  doubt. `ideas.md` notes that Ep. 01 is the hook episode which establishes the
  data-volume premise so Eps. 02–15 can go straight to their mechanism.

## Wording decisions (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW)

1. **"about nine hundred thousand"** for "nearly 900 000" — spoken as words
   because Kokoro handles long numerals poorly; the exact figure is on screen.
2. **"eight point six seven million"** spoken; `8,670,000` on screen with its
   source. B11 says "nearly nine million", which is a rounding *up* of 8.67M and
   is flagged here as the one place the voice is looser than the screen.
3. **"about ninety nine percent accurate"** is qualified in the narration by
   "against confident volunteer answers" — the paper's own condition. Dropping
   that qualifier would turn a conditional result into a blanket accuracy claim.
4. **"the twenty fourteen competition"** rather than naming Kaggle in the voice;
   the platform, dates and authors are on screen in B08.
5. **B07's 63/37 split is invented and marked as such** — spoken as "say sixty
   three of a hundred", captioned on screen as an illustrative split. It is the
   only non-published number in the reel.
6. **"it does not travel"** is a plain-language rendering of "distributional
   mismatch"; the technical phrase appears on screen in B10 alongside it.
7. **No hyphens, dashes or colons in narration**, carried over from the series
   convention — it measurably improves Kokoro's delivery.
