## Why this topic gets this treatment

`ideas.md` lists Astronomy topic 04 as "Galaxy Zoo + CNNs sorting millions of
galaxy images by shape." The obvious film — *there are too many galaxies, so AI
sorts them* — is the wrong one twice over. First, the series notes in `ideas.md`
say Ep. 01 already carries the data-volume premise so later episodes can go
straight to mechanism. Second, that film has no idea in it.

The idea in Galaxy Zoo is stranger and better: **the label is not a class, it is
a distribution.** Volunteers disagree about whether a faint bar is a bar, and
instead of majority-voting the disagreement away, the project keeps it and trains
the network to predict it. That is a transferable lesson about supervised
learning, and it is what this episode teaches.

## What the viewer should be able to do afterwards

1. **Say what is actually being predicted.** Not "spiral" — the *fraction of
   humans who would answer spiral*. If they come away thinking Galaxy Zoo is a
   classifier that outputs a class name, the episode failed.
2. **Reproduce the loop.** Decision tree → many volunteers per galaxy → vote
   fractions → CNN trained on the fractions → active learning returns only the
   uncertain galaxies to people.
3. **Explain one architectural decision from physics.** A galaxy has no
   preferred orientation, so the network is fed the same image at several
   rotations through shared weights. Symmetry in the problem became structure in
   the model.
4. **Name both ceilings.** The model cannot be more right than the crowd it
   predicts; and it does not transfer between surveys, because the pixel
   distribution shifts.
5. **Transfer it.** Apply soft labels, calibration and active learning to their
   own disagreeing-annotator problem — which is what the handoff asks for.

## Cognitive-load / pacing notes

- **One new element per beat.** B05 (the tree) and B06 (the fraction) are split
  deliberately: "you are asked a sequence of questions" and "the answer is a
  proportion" are two different ideas, and merging them is what makes most
  explanations of Galaxy Zoo incomprehensible.
- **B03 is a concrete-before-abstract beat.** The viewer sees the five
  morphologies before any mechanism, so later talk of "bars" and "arms" has a
  referent.
- **B02 is the advance organizer**, stating the whole idea in one breath —
  including the punchline ("predict the disagreement") — so the body lands
  against a whole the viewer already holds.
- **Consolidation floors** (duration-planner): the `mechanism` beats B06, B07,
  B08 and B10 are the longest by design; each new step has to integrate with
  steps still active in working memory.
- **B07 holds the 63/37 grid on screen** long enough to be counted before it
  collapses into the fraction — the collapse is the teaching moment.

## Register check (Pragmatist)

- Leads with **method** (B06), gives a **worked example** (B07), names the

  source are on screen in B09.
- **The ~99% accuracy figure is conditional** — it is measured against
  *confident* volunteer classifications, and the narration says so.
