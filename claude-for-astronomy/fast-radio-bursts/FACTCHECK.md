## Provenance of this verification — read first

Claims were verified **from primary sources during this build (2026-08-16)** —
published papers and instrument-description papers, not summaries. Each row
carries the figure as the source states it and the URL it came from.

Two things a reviewer should know:

1. **Every number in the narration is a published figure.** There is no invented
   number anywhere in this cut — unlike Ep. 04, which carried one illustrative
   vote split.
2. **Every dynamic spectrum is synthetic**, generated by `assets/gen_frb.py`
   directly from the dispersion law. They illustrate signal morphology; no claim
   rests on them, and the scenes caption them.

## Verified, then deliberately NOT used

- **The Lorimer burst** (the 2007 discovery paper, in archival Parkes data from
  2001). Well established, but the search pass did not surface a primary source
  quotation for the specific dates, and the film does not need an origin story.
  Left out rather than asserted on memory.
- **FRB 20200428 from magnetar SGR 1935+2154**, the Galactic burst that tied FRBs
  to magnetars. Genuinely important, and cut: this episode is about the detection
  pipeline, not about what FRBs are, and a progenitor beat would have pulled the
  film into a different subject.
- **"Only ~3% of the sky-days have been searched" style scarcity framings** —
  omitted because they date quickly.

## Wording decisions (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW)

1. **"about twenty seconds"** for the decision window is the *usable* buffer
   (≈20 s) after the ~14 s pipeline latency is subtracted from the 35.5 s ring,
   which is how the source frames it. B06 states the 35.5 s ring on screen so
   both numbers are visible and the relationship is not hidden.
2. **"about one and a half petabytes a day"** is the paper's own figure. An
   earlier draft carried a derived bits-per-second figure taken from a search
   summary; it was arithmetically wrong and was dropped rather than corrected,
   since the paper's own unit is clearer anyway.
3. **"one hundred billion signal to noise values every second"** renders 10¹¹ in
   words; the exponent form is on screen.
4. **"about a hundred thousand candidates a day"** renders the source's ~10⁵.
5. **"for years"** for the peryton mystery, rather than a specific span — the
   sources establish that perytons were a long-standing puzzle resolved in 2015
   without my having verified a precise start year.
6. **"better than ninety nine and a half percent recall"** keeps the source's
   qualifier ("on the test dataset") in the on-screen citation rather than
   inflating it to a general accuracy claim.
7. **No hyphens, dashes or colons in narration**, per the series convention — it
   measurably improves Kokoro's delivery.
